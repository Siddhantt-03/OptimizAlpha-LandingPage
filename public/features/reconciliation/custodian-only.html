Calder Family Office: eight days of reconciliation from custodian files

This walkthrough shows how OptimizAlpha reconciles a portfolio when the custodian is the only source of data. It follows Calder Family Office, a fictional GBP portfolio held at a single custodian, through eight business days from 21 to 30 September 2026.

Each morning OA receives the custodian's holdings, transactions, cash statement, corporate-action notices and settlement-fail report. OA checks these files against each other, against yesterday's approved positions and against its own prices and exchange rates. It traces every difference to its cause and releases the day's data only once each difference is matched or approved.

Over the eight days, 18 common data problems occur, from a dividend missing from the transaction file to a stale custodian price. Every figure on this page is calculated live by the OA reconciliation engine.

The data

One external source, the custodian, sends files each morning. OA checks them against two things it already holds.

1 · Custodian files (the only source)

Five files from Northgate: holdings with price and FX; the transaction file; the cash statement with every settled entry; corporate-action notices; and a settlement-fail report. Most scenarios now plant errors inside these files: a row left out of the transaction file, a duplicate, a keying error, a stale price, and a balance with no entry.

2 · OA reference data

OA's security master (identifiers, multipliers) and its vendor prices and FX. Several scenarios plant errors here: a bad multiplier load, an inverted FX rate and a stale price.

3 · OA released book

Yesterday's approved and released positions and settled cash. This is the opening balance for today. It only moves forward after a day is signed off.

About the data. Listed securities use their real ISINs and approximate price levels. Daily prices and FX are generated by a fixed formula for illustration, so they are not market data. The corporate-action and bond names (Kestrel, Alder, Birchfield, Fennick, Ostrander) are fictional, with test identifiers starting ZZTEST, so no real company is shown with an invented event. Dividend amounts are illustrative.
How OA checks a single source. OA runs three cross-checks on the custodian's own data. Holdings must equal yesterday's approved positions plus the custodian's transactions. Every cash statement entry must have a matching transaction, and the statement must add up. Each transaction must be internally consistent (quantity × price = gross). OA also tests the custodian's prices and exchange rates against its own data, so the custodian is never assumed to be right.

The daily cycle

Every day runs the same 15 stages. Stages 1-11 are deterministic rules. Stage 12 is the AI investigator, whose output is simulated in this page (explained below). Stages 13-14 need people, and stage 15 is a hard gate in front of the compute engine.

If you also receive manager files

When the investment manager also sends a trade and accounting file, OA has a second, independent source and compares the two line by line. The table shows how each scenario is caught in that setup and what changes when the custodian is the only source. Most scenarios are caught the same way or with an equivalent check. Two (S02, S16) can be proven outright from the manager file, rather than being capped at MEDIUM confidence and confirmed with the custodian.

The scenario labels (S01-S18) are display tags. The engine's matching, explainers and rules never read them; they are only used to highlight rows and to fill in this table.

The last two columns show why the gate matters even with a single trusted source. "Unreconciled" is the return OA would have computed if the custodian files had been loaded as received and valued with OA's reference data as it stood without reconciliation. Some errors do not move NAV on the day they happen. A duplicated sale, for example, removes the position but adds an equal receivable. Those errors still misstate holdings, exposure and attribution until they are fixed.

Simplifications in this example: there is one custodian account; trades price at the day's close; accrued interest on bonds is left out; returns use a simple daily Dietz formula with external flows at mid-day, standing in for OA's configured method. The AI investigator's text is generated from evidence by templates in this page. In production it comes from the investigator agent, and the same deterministic validator shown here checks it. User decisions follow a fixed playbook so that the example can be repeated. Escalations to the custodian are answered the same day, with the record the custodian should have sent.
